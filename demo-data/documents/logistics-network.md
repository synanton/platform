# Synanton Logistics Network Overview

## Network Architecture

The Synanton logistics network spans North America and Europe, connecting manufacturing facilities operated by suppliers including Globex Manufacturing and Initech Ltd with Acme Corp's distribution centers and end-customer delivery points. The network is organized into three tiers: inbound freight from tier-one suppliers to regional consolidation hubs, inter-hub trunk moves between consolidation centers, and last-mile distribution to customer facilities.

## Key Facilities

**Consolidation Hubs**
- Columbus, OH - Primary inbound hub for Globex Manufacturing shipments; 180,000 sq ft cross-dock facility
- Mississauga, ON - Co-located with Initech Ltd's VMI hub; handles both road and air freight for time-critical releases
- Atlanta, GA - Southeast regional distribution center serving Acme Corp's automotive customer base
- Krakow, PL - European hub adjacent to Initech Ltd's Poland facility; connects to pan-European carrier network

## Transportation Modes and Carriers

Inbound shipments from Globex Manufacturing to the Columbus hub move predominantly by dedicated truckload, with a contracted cycle of three departures per week from each Ohio facility. Time-sensitive releases utilize less-than-truckload (LTL) services with next-day transit capability for destinations within a 500-mile radius.

Initech Ltd's harness and PCBA shipments to Acme Corp production lines move via a milk-run service operating on a six-hour frequency during peak production days, with emergency air freight capability available for critical shortages.

International shipments between the Mississauga hub and the Krakow facility move by ocean freight for standard releases (28-day transit) and by air for expedited requirements, with a target of limiting air freight to less than 5% of annual volume.

## Continuous Improvement Initiatives

The logistics team is piloting a dynamic routing optimization system that adjusts carrier selection and route choice in real time based on current capacity availability, weather forecasts, and traffic conditions. Early results from the Columbus-to-Atlanta lane show a 4% reduction in transit time variability and a 2.1% reduction in freight cost per unit.
